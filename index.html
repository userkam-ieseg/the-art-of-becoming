01 / INTRODUCTION

A Little About Me.

I'm Kamilia, a business student at IÉSEG. I've always enjoyed learning through experience. Whether it's speaking to a customer, working on a group project or figuring something out on my own, I tend to understand things better once I actually try them.

My experiences at MEDIBOX France, Lacoste and Pandora showed me that communicating with people is not just about finding the right words. It is also about listening, adapting and understanding what the other person needs. Telephone prospecting was especially challenging because I had to build a connection with someone who had not asked to speak to me.

I value independence because I enjoy taking responsibility for my work. However, I am also learning that being independent does not mean doing everything alone. My next goal is to gain experience abroad, improve my confidence in an international environment and explore my interest in cybersecurity sales.

always evolving ↗

02 / RELATIONSHIP SKILLS

Growth Is Not Always Linear.

One thing I realised during this module is that knowing what I should do and actually doing it are two different things. I can understand how to manage stress or organise my time, but applying those techniques when I'm under pressure is where the real learning happens.

I have also started paying more attention to the way I study. Techniques such as Pomodoro and timeboxing help me organise my revision sessions, but the biggest change has been learning to test myself without looking at the answers. I realised that recognising a solution is not the same as being able to find it independently.

✦

Self-Awareness

I used to think that understanding a corrected maths exercise meant I knew how to do it. Then I'd sit down the next day, without the correction in front of me, and realise I was completely stuck.

Instead of simply rereading corrections, I now try to solve exercises on my own and come back to them after a few days. This has helped me identify what I genuinely understand and what I still need to practise. I am also becoming more comfortable with asking for help when I need it.

♡

Resilience

Getting rejected on the phone was never particularly enjoyable, especially when it happened several times in a row. But I slowly learned not to take every "no" personally.

I experienced something similar while preparing for my driving licence. Having to retake the test was frustrating, but each attempt taught me something. Looking back, I think resilience is less about never feeling discouraged and more about finding the motivation to try again.

↗

Confidence

I feel comfortable being given responsibilities and working independently. What I'm still learning is how to speak up and suggest something before someone asks me to.

Securing an appointment during my internship was an important moment because it showed me that my preparation could lead to a concrete result. My internship feedback also helped me recognise that taking initiative is an area I can improve. I want to become more comfortable sharing ideas and trusting my judgement.

03 / COMMUNICATION

The Power of People.

Through my experiences in sales and group projects, I've realised that communication is not just about speaking confidently. It's also about understanding who I'm talking to and adapting the way I express myself.

At MEDIBOX France, telephone prospecting taught me how important listening really is. I couldn't rely on facial expressions or body language, so I had to pay attention to people's tone, reactions and questions. I also learned that sometimes, listening carefully is more effective than trying to convince someone immediately.

"Communication is more than just speaking."

Working on group presentations at IÉSEG showed me another side of communication. When several people have different ideas and working styles, being able to explain your opinion matters, but so does making space for others.

I'm naturally comfortable taking responsibility for my own work. What I'm still developing is the ability to share responsibilities, trust other people's approaches and express my ideas more confidently within a team.

04 / BUSINESS GAME

Thinking Beyond the Obvious.

The Business Game helped me think about how different business decisions connect. Marketing, finance and strategy cannot always be considered separately: a choice in one area can influence the others.

ILLUSTRATIVE BUSINESS SCENARIO

◎

Strategy

Imagine a fashion brand competing in a crowded market. Rather than lowering prices to attract everyone, a possible strategy would be to focus on quality, brand identity and customer experience.

This connects to The Art and Science of Marketing, where positioning and understanding customer needs are central to building a strong brand.

↗

Decision-Making

A possible challenge would be deciding whether to invest more in marketing or keep expenses low to protect profitability.

A larger marketing budget might attract customers, but it would also create costs without guaranteeing sales. Concepts from Finance help explain why a promising marketing decision can still involve financial risks.

✳

Teamwork

In a business simulation, one team member might prioritise growth while another focuses on controlling costs. Discussing the reasoning behind both positions can lead to a more balanced decision.

This connects to Relationship Skills: contributing to a team means sharing ideas, listening and sometimes reconsidering your first opinion.

The main lesson of this example is that business decisions require analysis, adaptability and an understanding of how different functions work together.

05 / WEB DEVELOPMENT

Learning to Create.

Before discovering web development, I mainly thought about websites from a user's perspective. I paid attention to their design, colours and overall atmosphere without necessarily thinking about the work behind them.

Learning about HTML and CSS helped me understand how a website is structured and how visual choices can completely change the experience of a visitor.

What interests me most about web design is the creative side. I enjoy the idea of building something that reflects a personality through typography, colours and layout.

I also discovered that creating a website requires patience. A small mistake in the code can affect the final result, and finding the problem means checking your work carefully rather than giving up immediately.

This experience has encouraged me to become more curious about digital tools. Even though I don't necessarily want to become a web developer, I can see how understanding technology could be valuable in my future career.

06 / WHAT'S NEXT?

My Next Chapter.

If there's one thing I've learned about myself, it's that I grow the most when I step outside my comfort zone.

My experiences have helped me become more independent and adaptable, but they've also shown me what I still need to work on. I want to become more confident when taking initiative, more consistent in the way I organise my work and more comfortable collaborating with different personalities.

One of my next goals is to complete an international internship in an English-speaking environment. I want to discover a new professional culture, challenge myself and develop skills that I wouldn't necessarily gain by staying in a familiar environment.

In the longer term, I'm interested in building a career in cybersecurity sales, where I could combine my interest in business, communication and technology.

I don't have everything figured out yet, and I think that's part of the process. What matters to me is continuing to learn, trying new things and becoming someone who is not afraid to take opportunities.

I'm not a finished product. And that's exactly the point.
