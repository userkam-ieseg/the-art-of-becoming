01 / INTRODUCTION

A Little About Me.

I'm Kamilia, a business student at IÉSEG. I've always enjoyed learning through experience. Whether it's speaking to a customer, working on a group project or figuring something out on my own, I tend to understand things better once I actually try them.

My experiences at MEDIBOX France, Lacoste and Pandora showed me that communicating with people is not just about finding the right words. It's also about listening, adapting and understanding what the other person needs. Telephone prospecting was especially challenging because I had to build a connection with someone who hadn't asked to speak to me.

I enjoy working independently and being trusted with responsibilities. At the same time, I've realised that independence doesn't mean having to do everything alone. Working with different people has taught me to be more open to other approaches, even when they're different from mine.

I'm interested in business, communication and the way technology is changing professional relationships. I speak French and English, and I'm also learning Spanish. Discovering other cultures is something that genuinely interests me, which is one of the reasons I'd love to complete an internship abroad.

I don't think I have one fixed version of who I want to become. My interests have evolved, and I'm still discovering what suits me. What I do know is that I want a career where I can keep learning, meeting people and challenging myself.

always evolving ↗

02 / RELATIONSHIP SKILLS

Growth Is Not Always Linear.

One thing I realised during this module is that knowing what I should do and actually doing it are two different things. I can understand how to manage stress or organise my time, but applying those techniques when I'm under pressure is where the real learning happens.

I have also started paying more attention to the way I study. Techniques such as Pomodoro and timeboxing help me organise my revision sessions, but the biggest change has been learning to test myself without looking at the answers. I realised that recognising a solution isn't the same as being able to find it independently.

✦

Self-Awareness

I used to think that understanding a corrected maths exercise meant I knew how to do it. Then I'd sit down the next day, without the correction in front of me, and realise I was completely stuck.

Instead of simply rereading corrections, I now try to solve exercises on my own and come back to them after a few days. It helps me see what I've actually understood and what I still need to practise. I've also learned that asking for help is sometimes the smartest thing I can do.

♡

Resilience

Getting rejected on the phone during my internship wasn't particularly enjoyable, especially when it happened several times in a row. But I slowly learned not to take every "no" personally.

I experienced something similar while preparing for my driving licence. Having to retake the test was frustrating, but each attempt taught me something. Looking back, resilience isn't about never feeling discouraged. It's about finding the motivation to try again, even when things don't go as planned.

↗

Confidence

I feel comfortable being given responsibilities and working independently. What I'm still learning is how to speak up and suggest something before someone asks me to.

Securing an appointment during my internship was an important moment because it showed me that my preparation could lead to a real result. My internship feedback also helped me realise that taking initiative is something I can improve. I want to trust my judgement more and stop waiting until I feel completely ready.

WHAT I'M LEARNING

Progress doesn't always look impressive from the outside. Sometimes it's simply being able to do something today that felt impossible a few weeks ago.

03 / COMMUNICATION & TEAMWORK

The Power of People.

Through my experiences in sales and group projects, I've realised that communication isn't just about speaking confidently. It's also about understanding who I'm talking to and adapting the way I express myself. Something that works with one person might not work at all with another.

At MEDIBOX France, telephone prospecting taught me how important listening really is. I couldn't rely on facial expressions or body language, so I had to pay attention to people's tone, reactions and questions. Sometimes, the person on the phone wasn't interested, and I had to accept that. Other times, asking the right question made the conversation much easier.

"Communication is more than just speaking."

Working on presentations at IÉSEG showed me another side of communication. Group projects can be challenging because everyone has different ideas, schedules and ways of working. I like things to be organised, so I sometimes find it difficult when people don't approach a task the same way I do.

I've realised that being part of a team also means accepting those differences. When preparing a presentation, for example, dividing the work clearly helps everyone understand their responsibilities. But checking in with each other is just as important, because having five separate pieces of work doesn't automatically create one good presentation.

My retail experiences at Lacoste and Pandora also taught me to adapt my communication. Some customers wanted detailed explanations, while others preferred to look around independently. Learning to recognise those differences helped me become more attentive and comfortable in professional conversations.

I still want to become more confident when expressing disagreement or proposing a new idea. I don't want to be the person who stays quiet just because someone else seems more certain. At the same time, I want to remain someone who genuinely listens instead of simply waiting for their turn to speak.

listen first, speak with purpose

04 / BUSINESS GAME

Thinking Beyond the Obvious.

Studying business at IÉSEG has made me realise how connected different subjects really are. Marketing, finance and strategy might be taught separately, but in a company, decisions in one area can influence everything else.

What I find interesting about business simulations is that they force us to think beyond theory. It's easy to say that a company should increase sales or improve its image. It's much harder to decide how to do that while keeping costs under control and considering competitors.

AN ILLUSTRATIVE BUSINESS CASE

A hypothetical fashion brand facing competition, pricing decisions and limited financial resources.

◎

01. Strategy

Imagine managing a fashion brand in a competitive market. One option would be to lower prices to attract more customers. Another would be to focus on quality, brand identity and customer experience.

I would be more interested in the second approach. From my experiences in retail, I've seen how presentation, service and the feeling associated with a brand can influence a purchase.

This connects to The Art and Science of Marketing, especially positioning and understanding what customers actually value.

↗

02. Decisions

The next challenge would be deciding how much money to invest in marketing. A larger budget could increase brand awareness, but it wouldn't guarantee more sales.

Concepts from Finance and Accounting become useful here. Revenue alone doesn't tell us whether a business is performing well. We also need to consider expenses, profitability and the resources available.

I think this is what makes business decisions interesting: an idea can sound exciting but still be financially unrealistic.

✳

03. Teamwork

In this kind of simulation, I can imagine one team member focusing on increasing sales while another is more concerned about costs.

Instead of immediately choosing one side, the team would need to compare both arguments and agree on its priorities.

This links directly to Relationship Skills. Being involved in a team isn't about always having the final word. It's about contributing, listening and being willing to reconsider your first opinion.

MY MAIN TAKEAWAY

This example brings together several things I've studied at IÉSEG. Marketing helps us understand customers, finance helps us evaluate what is realistic, and teamwork helps turn different opinions into a decision.

It also connects to something I'm personally working on: being confident enough to share my ideas while staying open to other people's perspectives.

05 / WEB DEVELOPMENT

Learning to Create.

Before discovering web development, I mainly thought about websites from a user's perspective. I noticed their colours, typography and overall atmosphere, but I didn't really think about how everything worked behind the screen.

Learning about HTML and CSS helped me understand the difference between structure and appearance. HTML organises the content of a page, while CSS changes how that content looks. Something as simple as adjusting the spacing or choosing a different font can completely change the feeling of a website.

The creative side is definitely what interests me most. I've always liked things that have a strong visual identity, whether it's fashion, branding or interior design. So the idea of creating a website that reflects a personality is something I find genuinely exciting.

I also discovered that web development requires patience. A missing bracket, a spelling mistake in a class name or a CSS rule in the wrong place can affect the final result. When something doesn't look right, the solution isn't always obvious.

This portfolio is also an opportunity to think about how I want to present myself. I didn't want something that looked like a traditional CV. I wanted a more editorial and colourful atmosphere, with strong typography and a design that feels both creative and professional.

Even though I don't necessarily see myself becoming a web developer, I think understanding digital tools will be useful in my future career. I'm particularly interested in cybersecurity sales, and being comfortable with technology would help me communicate with both technical teams and clients.

06 / WHAT'S NEXT?

My Next Chapter.

If there's one thing I've learned about myself, it's that I grow the most when I step outside my comfort zone.

My experiences have helped me become more independent and adaptable, but they've also shown me what I still need to work on. I want to become more confident when taking initiative, more consistent in the way I organise my work and more comfortable collaborating with different personalities.

One of my next goals is to complete an international internship in an English-speaking professional environment. I want to discover a new culture, meet people with different perspectives and prove to myself that I can adapt to an unfamiliar setting.

I also want to keep improving my language skills. Speaking French and English already gives me opportunities to communicate with different people, and learning Spanish is another way for me to become more comfortable internationally.

In the longer term, I'm interested in building a career in cybersecurity sales. What attracts me is the combination of business, communication and technology. I like the idea of understanding a client's problem and finding a solution that makes sense for them.

I know I still have a lot to learn about the technical side of cybersecurity, but that's also part of what motivates me. I'd like to develop my knowledge step by step, gain more professional experience and become someone who can work confidently in an international environment.

I don't have everything figured out yet, and I think that's normal. My goals might evolve as I gain experience. For now, what matters to me is staying curious, taking opportunities and not being afraid to start something new.
